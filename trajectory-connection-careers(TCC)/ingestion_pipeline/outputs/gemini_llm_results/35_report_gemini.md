# Baseline Trajectory Report

**Background:** An experienced Data Scientist with over a decade of analytical advisory experience (2008–2019) across marketing and research sectors, specializing in predictive modeling, exploratory data analysis, unstructured data mining, and translating quantitative findings into strategic plans for business stakeholders.
**Goal:** To continue advancing in a leadership role where predictive modeling and data analysis can drive strategic business growth and improvement (targeting Data Science Manager, Director of Business Analytics, or Lead Strategic Analytics Advisor).

## 1. Recommended Path

To transition into an executive or senior managerial analytics leadership role, the recommended sequence emphasizes closing career gaps, mastering modern data science infrastructure, and validating strategic delivery:

1. **Re-enter and Modernize Technical Stack (Months 1–3):**
* Update the analytical toolkit to current industry standards (Python 3.11+, PySpark, modern SQL, Git, and cloud notebooks via Databricks or AWS SageMaker).
* Translate previous predictive modeling methodologies into reproducible machine learning pipelines, formalizing past experience with concrete tooling and framework terminology (e.g., scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP/LIME for explainability).


2. **Demonstrate Business Impact & Productized Analytics (Months 4–8):**
* Lead projects focused on connecting predictive models directly to measurable business KPIs (e.g., customer lifetime value, churn prevention, uplift modeling, marketing mix modeling).
* Integrate modern data visualization and BI tools (Tableau, Power BI, Looker) with semantic layer architectures to build executive decision engines.


3. **Transition into People & Strategic Analytics Leadership (Months 9–12):**
* Establish analytics operating models: defining team OKRs, setting data quality and governance standards, and bridging technical data teams with executive and sales/marketing leadership.
* Target management tracks such as Manager/Associate Director of Strategic Analytics or Marketing Data Science Manager.



**Reasoning:** The candidate’s primary differentiator is an academic background in Communications combined with analytical practice, creating strong executive translation capabilities. Moving directly into technical staff/principal roles would be hindered by the lack of explicit engineering depth, whereas a strategic leadership track (Analytics Manager/Director) leverages their stakeholder advisory strengths.

## 2. Risk Analysis & Feasibility Flags

* **Significant Career Employment Gap:** The resume displays no formal employment listed after August 2019. A multi-year gap creates an immediate screening hurdle in technical recruiting that must be actively addressed (e.g., consulting, advisory work, professional sabbaticals, or continuous education).
* **High-Level / Vague Technical Descriptions:** The resume contains virtually no named technical frameworks, languages, or tools (omitting explicit mentions of Python, R, SQL, cloud platforms, or specific machine learning libraries). Descriptors such as "algorithmic and programming tools" or "analytical functions" read as non-technical to hiring managers.
* **Absence of Quantified Business Metrics:** Past accomplishments are presented purely qualitatively (e.g., "helped companies make decisions", "tracked performance"). Senior and leadership roles demand evidence of revenue uplift, operational savings, or efficiency metrics.
* **Non-STEM Academic Background:** A Bachelor of Communications degree is an asset for stakeholder engagement, but combined with generic technical bullet points, it exposes the profile to bias in automated screening systems prioritizing Computer Science, Statistics, or Applied Mathematics.
* **Feasibility Verdict:** Low feasibility for Lead Machine Learning Engineer or Staff Data Scientist roles; moderate-to-high feasibility for Analytics Manager, Product Analytics Lead, or Strategy/BI Director roles once the resume is rewritten with concrete tooling and recent experience is accounted for.

## 3. Experience Analysis & Skills to Acquire

**Technical Skills to Acquire:**

* **Modern Core Data Stack:** Advanced SQL (window functions, query optimization), Python (pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels), and version control with Git.
* **Cloud Analytics & Lakehouse Platforms:** Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, or AWS (Athena, S3, Redshift).
* **Modern Experimentation & Causal Inference:** A/B testing statistical rigor, hypothesis testing, quasi-experimentation, and customer segmentation methods.
* **BI & Data Storytelling:** Modern dashboarding frameworks (Tableau, Power BI, Streamlit) integrated with warehouse-native modeling.

**Common Hurdles & Transition Struggles:**

* **Overcoming the "Black Box" Perception:** Explaining complex algorithmic models without defaulting to vague terminology; articulating precisely which model architectures (e.g., Random Forests, Logistic Regression, Gradient Boosting) were selected and why.
* **Navigating Technical Screening Loops:** Clearing live SQL assessments, system architecture evaluations, and take-home data challenges that have become standard in modern data science interviews.
* **Re-establishing Professional Momentum:** Re-framing the post-2019 period effectively to assure hiring teams of up-to-date industry familiarity and continuous engagement with emerging data technologies.

## 4. Mentor Discovery Strategy

**Target Mentor Profiles:**

* **Director of Business Analytics / Commercial Data Science:** A leader who manages hybrid teams of data scientists and business analysts, specializing in marketing, sales, or customer analytics.
* **Data Science Leader from a Non-Traditional Background:** A senior director or VP who entered data science with a humanities or social sciences degree, who can advise on positioning strong communication skills as a leadership superpower.

**Relevance & Focus Areas:**

* **Past Job Titles to Search:** *Senior Data Scientist -> Analytics Manager*, *Lead Commercial Analyst -> Director of Strategic Insights*, *Head of Business Intelligence & Data Science*.
* **Why They Are Relevant:** These mentors understand how to navigate non-technical screening filters, how to strategically address employment breaks, and how to position advisory and communications expertise to secure high-leverage data leadership roles.
