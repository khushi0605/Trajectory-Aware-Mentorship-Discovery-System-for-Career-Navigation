# Baseline Trajectory Report

**Background:** Andy Koch is a seasoned Core Java Developer with nearly two decades of server-side experience in high-performance financial systems, spanning market-making, execution technology, equities middle-office platforms, pricing and risk components, UNIX/Linux environments, and relational database systems (Sybase, SQL).
**Goal:** Andy aims to leverage his extensive experience in high-performance market-making technology and equities applications to take on a leadership role in software development, focusing on strategic initiatives and team management (targeting Engineering Manager, Lead Trading Systems Architect, or Director of Electronic Trading Technology).

## 1. Recommended Path

To transition from hands-on, support-coupled development into dedicated engineering management or trading systems architecture, the recommended progression balances technical modernization with strategic people and platform leadership:

1. **Modernize Core Low-Latency & Reactive Java Stack (Months 1–3):**
* Update core Java proficiencies to modern LTS versions (Java 17/21), integrating modern concurrency patterns (Virtual Threads/Project Loom, LMAX Disruptor, zero-allocation memory models).
* Shift from general server-side design toward explicit low-latency, high-throughput architectural patterns (e.g., Aeron, Chronicle Queue, FIX protocol engines).


2. **Step into Lead Architect / Scrum Lead Capacity (Months 4–8):**
* Formalize technical leadership by driving architecture decision records (ADRs) for equities/FX execution workflows, decoupling operational L3 support from core innovation.
* Lead sprint planning, cross-regional team coordination (onsite/offshore models), and define engineering excellence metrics (reducing operational debt, automating test harnesses, CI/CD with GitOps).


3. **Transition to Engineering Management & Strategic Governance (Months 9–12):**
* Assume formal management responsibilities: 1-on-1 career development, performance reviews, resource allocation, and budget/vendor management.
* Partner with front-office business stakeholders, trading desk heads, and compliance officers to shape the multi-year technology roadmap for middle/front-office platforms.



**Reasoning:** Andy has deep capital markets domain expertise (Equities, FX, Commodities, Market Making) and foundational people management experience from earlier roles. To re-enter leadership, he needs to break out of the "Level 3 application support" positioning and demonstrate formal strategic delivery and team leadership over high-value trading infrastructure.

## 2. Risk Analysis & Feasibility Flags

* **Title Inversion & Downward Mobility Signaling:** The resume shows an anomalous title progression: *Senior Core Java Developer (2011–2015)* followed by *Intermediate Core Java Developer (2016–Present)*. This downward trajectory will raise immediate screening flags for leadership and management positions unless clearly contextualized (e.g., organizational title normalization, contract tiering).
* **Heavy Emphasis on Level 3 Operational Support:** A significant portion of the current role emphasizes L3 support, ticket investigation, and debugging. This masks engineering depth and can pigeonhole Andy as a maintenance/support engineer rather than a strategic technical leader or architect.
* **Absence of Modern Distributed & Cloud Infrastructure:** There is no explicit mention of modern messaging backbones (Kafka), cloud environments (AWS, GCP), container platforms (Docker, Kubernetes), or modern low-latency frameworks, leaving his profile anchored in legacy on-premises paradigms (Sybase, shell scripts).
* **Feasibility Verdict:** High feasibility for Team Lead / Technical Lead roles within traditional investment banks and trading firms; moderate feasibility for Software Engineering Manager (SEM) or Director roles until recent support responsibilities are reframed into strategic delivery and management.
