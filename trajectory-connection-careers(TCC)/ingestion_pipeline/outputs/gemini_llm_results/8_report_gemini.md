# Baseline Trajectory Report

**Background:** Senior SQL Database Developer with experience in database administration, design, development, and security, with a strong focus on performance optimization and reporting.
**Goal:** Aspire to lead a team of database developers and contribute to the development of innovative database solutions.

---

## 1. Recommended Path

* **Phase 1: Modernize Cloud Data Platform & Modern Architecture Patterns (Months 1–12)**
* **Objective:** Transition traditional on-premise MS SQL Server and SSRS patterns to cloud-native database architectures and distributed data technologies.
* **Focus Areas:** Modern cloud databases (Azure SQL Managed Instance, AWS RDS/Aurora PostgreSQL), cloud data warehousing (Snowflake, Azure Synapse/Fabric), and database CI/CD automation using tools like Flyway, Liquibase, or SSDT.
* **Milestone:** Architect and lead the migration of a mission-critical transactional or analytical database from on-premise to a managed cloud environment, establishing automated schema migrations and performance monitoring baselines.


* **Phase 2: Formalize Technical Lead Ownership (Months 12–24)**
* **Objective:** Elevate current informal mentorship and estimation duties into formal technical leadership and architectural governance.
* **Focus Areas:** Technical roadmap ownership, database design review boards, cross-functional alignment with backend and analytics engineering teams, and standardizing data security/governance protocols.
* **Milestone:** Serve as the Lead Database Engineer across a pod of 4–6 database developers/data engineers, authoring Architectural Decision Records (ADRs) and overseeing the rollout of modern database solutions.


* **Phase 3: Transition to Data Engineering / Database Team Lead (Year 2+)**
* **Objective:** Assume formal management and strategic leadership of a database/data engineering team.
* **Focus Areas:** Agile team leadership, sprint planning, hiring, performance coaching, vendor management, and enterprise-wide data strategy.
* **Milestone:** Step into a Database Development Manager or Lead Data Platform Architect role, leading the delivery of scalable, cloud-first database solutions.



**Reasoning:** The candidate possesses two decades of continuous, progressive experience (2006 to present) spanning SQL database development, administration, security, and performance tuning. They already perform foundational leadership duties—mentoring developers, scoping estimations, and driving architectural documentation. Because their core database fundamentals and tenure are mature, the main lever to achieving their goal of leading a team on innovative solutions is modernizing their stack into cloud-native and modern lakehouse/distributed paradigms.

## 2. Risk Analysis & Feasibility Flags

* **Resume Presentation & Phrasing Disconnect:** Similar to template-derived resumes, several bullet points read like passive job descriptions ("Great at writing SQL...", "DB2 knowledge is a great", "Performs a variety of tasks. May provide consultation..."). This lack of first-person, metric-driven achievements weakens the candidate's executive and technical leadership profile.
* **Legacy Tooling Anchor (SSRS & On-Premise Focus):** Heavy reliance on SSRS reports and traditional MS SQL administration poses a risk in organizations looking for modern business intelligence and data platforms (e.g., Power BI, Snowflake, Databricks, Looker).
* **Vague Cloud Exposure:** While the resume states working with DevOps to set up database servers "in the cloud," there is little detail on cloud-native capabilities (e.g., auto-scaling, serverless databases, Infrastructure as Code via Terraform, distributed read replicas).
* **Informal vs. Formal Leadership Scope:** Mentoring and providing estimates demonstrate strong senior-level behaviors, but there is no documented record of formal people management, sprint ownership, or resolving complex inter-team delivery conflicts.

## 3. Experience Analysis & Skills to Acquire

**Technical Skills to Acquire:**

* **Modern Cloud Data Platforms:** AWS Aurora (PostgreSQL/MySQL), Azure SQL, and distributed analytical engines (Snowflake, Google BigQuery, or Databricks Lakehouse).
* **Database DevOps & Version Control:** Automating database deployments via GitOps, Liquibase, Flyway, and integrating database testing into CI/CD pipelines (GitHub Actions, GitLab CI).
* **Modern Reporting & Analytical Semantics:** Transitioning from SSRS to enterprise semantic modeling tools like Power BI (DAX, Tabular models) or dbt (data build tool) for data warehouse transformations.
* **Modern Security & Compliance Automation:** Automated data masking, row-level security policies, encryption-in-transit/at-rest, and governance frameworks (e.g., Microsoft Purview, Immuta).

**Common Hurdles & Transition Struggles:**

* **Letting Go of Hands-On Execution:** Senior database developers often struggle to delegate critical schema designs and query tuning, leading to bottlenecks instead of building team capacity through guided reviews.
* **Navigating the Cloud Paradigm Shift:** Moving from a traditional DBA mindset (manual indexing, hardware-level optimization, server-bound configs) to cloud-managed patterns (provisioned IOPS, serverless scaling, elasticity) requires unlearning legacy infrastructure constraints.
* **Strategic Communication:** Translating deep technical database debt and performance bottlenecks into quantifiable business impacts (infrastructure cost savings, reduced customer latency) when speaking with non-technical stakeholders.

## 4. Mentor Discovery Strategy

The candidate should seek mentors who have navigated the modernization of enterprise database architectures and established themselves as engineering leaders:

* **Target Profiles & Job Titles:**
* **Head of Data Platform / Director of Data Engineering:** A leader who manages teams building modern transactional and analytical data platforms, offering insight on hiring, team structuring, and enterprise data roadmap planning.
* **Principal Database Architect (Cloud/SaaS):** A veteran individual contributor who transitioned from traditional SQL Server/Oracle administration to modern distributed cloud databases, able to guide architectural modernization.
* **Engineering Manager (Data Infrastructure / Database Services):** A manager who made the leap from senior database developer to people leadership, offering tactical advice on performance management, delegation, and modern engineering management practices.



**Relevance:** These mentors will help the candidate elevate their extensive 20-year database background, structure their leadership narrative to move beyond tactical tasks, and adopt the modern cloud architectures necessary to lead high-performing teams delivering innovative solutions.
